# CalcPhi — Brand Voice Guide

## The One-Line Version

Speak like a knowledgeable friend who happens to have the right credentials — direct, specific, honest about trade-offs, never selling anything.

## Tone Attributes

**Direct.** Lead with the answer. The definition block opens with the clearest possible one-sentence statement of what the tool or concept does. Do not build up to the point.

**Specific.** Replace vague claims with numbers. Not "TDS is deducted at a relatively low rate" but "TDS is deducted at 10% once your FD interest exceeds ₹40,000 in a year." If a rate or threshold has changed recently, say when it changed.

**Honest about trade-offs.** When a financial product has a downside, name it plainly. CalcPhi does not soften inconvenient truths.

**Respectful of the reader's intelligence.** The audience can read a table, follow a worked example, and grasp a formula if it is presented cleanly. Do not over-explain. Do not hedge every sentence. Do not repeat what a table already shows in the paragraph below it.

**Sceptical of product marketing without being cynical.** Present the mechanism clearly and let the reader decide.

## What CalcPhi Does Not Sound Like

- **Not a bank brochure.** No "flexible and convenient solutions tailored to your needs."
- **Not a legal disclaimer.** Content has a disclaimer at the bottom; the article body is not written defensively.
- **Not a listicle farm.** No "10 Amazing Tips." Do "Five Legal Ways" with each method explained specifically, including the mechanism and the limit.
- **Not breathlessly urgent.** No "Act NOW before the deadline!"
- **Not padded.** No transitional filler ("Now that we've understood X, let's move on to Y").

## Banned words and phrases

- "in today's fast-paced world"
- "leverage" (when used metaphorically)
- "synergy", "game-changer", "unlock"
- "seamless", "holistic", "empower"
- "It depends" as an FAQ opener — always give the direct answer first

## Structural Voice Patterns

**Definition blocks** open with a plain declarative sentence, then context and nuance. Never open with a question ("Have you ever wondered...") or a hedge ("It depends on...").

**Worked examples** use named individuals with specific numbers. Show the calculation. Label the result. State the implication in one sentence.

**Comparison tables** use plain column headers, no decorative emoji, no "Winner!" callouts. Add a one-sentence below-the-table takeaway only when genuinely non-obvious.

**FAQ answers** start with a direct answer in the first sentence, then add nuance. "It depends on your tax bracket — if you're in the 30% slab, your TDS credit of 10% still leaves 20% payable at assessment" is correct.

**Section headings** are descriptive and specific: "The Accrual Rule: Why Cumulative FD Tax Surprises People" not "Tax Timing."

## India-Specific Voice Rules

- **Number format:** ₹1,00,000 (Indian system), ₹10 lakh, ₹1 crore — never ₹100,000
- **Tax years:** AY 2026-27 / FY 2025-26 — both, on first mention
- **Rates that change:** always note the "as of" date or the source
- **Named individuals in examples:** Rahul, Priya, Vikram, Ananya — realistic Indian names
- **Amounts:** Use ₹80,000/month basic, ₹10,000/month SIP, ₹5 lakh loan — not round global amounts
- **Regulators to cite by name:** SEBI, RBI, CBDT, AMFI, PFRDA, IRDAI, DICGC, ICAI
- **Regulatory sections:** Cite by name — Section 80C, Section 10(10D), 80CCD(1B), Section 44AD
- **Default tax regime:** New regime is the default from FY 2023-24 for salaried employees
- **Grounded in Indian reality:** Reference CIBIL, SIPs, FDs, PPF by full name on first mention

## Australia-Specific Voice Rules

- **Currency:** A$50,000 on first mention in a mixed context; $50,000 thereafter (context is clear)
- **Tax years:** 2025-26 financial year (not FY notation — Australians say "the 2025-26 year")
- **Regulators to cite by name:** ATO, ASIC, APRA, AFCA, Services Australia, Centrelink
- **Super facts:** Always use current SG rate (11.5% for 2024-25, 12% from 1 July 2025), current concessional cap ($30,000), non-concessional cap ($120,000)
- **Named individuals in examples:** Emma, Jack, Priya, Liam, Sophie — realistic Australian names
- **Amounts:** Use $85,000 salary, $500k super balance, $750k mortgage — realistic Australian amounts
- **Disclaimer wording for AU:** Reference ASIC general advice warning, not SEBI
- **Tone:** Slightly less formal than India content — Australians respond well to plain English and dry humour when appropriate

## What Changes by Article Type

**Calculator pages** are more functional — prose is tighter, examples shorter, FAQ is more how-to.

**Blog articles** follow the 8-section layout: definition block → feature image → main body (formula + worked examples + comparison tables) → infographic → FAQ section (5-6 questions) → disclaimer → author bio card.

**EEAT requirements:** Every article must have a named credentialed author, cite primary regulatory sources (not just third-party sites), and include at least one worked example with real numbers.
